Covenant Canopy Commons

Principles

The following principles establish the foundational commitments of Covenant Canopy Commons (CCC).

They are intended to guide the design of the network, its protocols, its software, and the communities that participate in it.

These principles are not intended to prescribe every possible community’s beliefs.

They establish the philosophical direction of the CCC project itself.

⸻

1. Human Dignity

Every person should be treated as a human being rather than merely as a resource, data source, customer, or commodity.

CCC should be designed with respect for:

* human dignity
* personal agency
* privacy
* freedom of conscience
* voluntary association
* personal responsibility

⸻

2. Freedom

Participants should have meaningful freedom to:

* create
* communicate
* associate
* disagree
* publish
* host
* develop
* participate
* leave
* build alternatives

Freedom should not depend upon permission from a single centralized authority whenever such dependence can reasonably be avoided.

⸻

3. Responsibility

Freedom and responsibility are inseparable.

Participants are responsible for their actions, creations, communications, and communities.

CCC should provide mechanisms that allow consequences to exist without requiring centralized control over the entire network.

⸻

4. Voluntary Association

People should generally be free to choose the communities and relationships in which they participate.

Communities should be able to establish their own standards.

Individuals should be able to leave communities whose standards they no longer wish to follow.

Communities should likewise be able to decline participation by individuals according to their published standards and applicable law.

⸻

5. Mutual Correction

CCC embraces the principle:

“Iron sharpens iron.”

People should be able to challenge ideas and challenge one another.

The network should support:

* criticism
* disagreement
* evidence
* correction
* debate
* revision
* reconciliation

Correction should not automatically require deletion or suppression.

⸻

6. Truth-Seeking

CCC should encourage the examination of claims rather than merely the amplification of popular claims.

Where technically practical, the system should support:

* provenance
* cryptographic signatures
* revision history
* citations
* evidence
* attribution
* competing interpretations

The network should make it possible to distinguish between:

* what was said
* who said it
* when it was said
* whether it was later corrected
* what evidence supports it
* what evidence challenges it

⸻

7. Transparency

Systems that exercise authority should provide meaningful transparency.

Where appropriate, users should be able to understand:

* what rules apply
* who established them
* how decisions are made
* why content was restricted within a community
* how appeals work
* how reputation is calculated
* how protocols operate

Transparency should not require disclosure of information that would compromise legitimate privacy or security.

⸻

8. Privacy

Privacy is an important component of personal autonomy.

CCC should minimize unnecessary collection of personal information.

The architecture should support:

* encryption
* pseudonyms
* anonymous participation where appropriate
* identity separation
* user-controlled data
* metadata minimization

Privacy should be designed into the architecture rather than added as an afterthought.

⸻

9. User Ownership

Users should have meaningful control over their:

* identities
* cryptographic keys
* content
* personal data
* domains
* community memberships
* locally stored information

A service provider should not automatically become the permanent owner of a user’s digital identity merely because the user used that service.

⸻

10. Portability

Users should be able to move.

A person’s identity, content, relationships, and community participation should not unnecessarily become permanently trapped within a single application or provider.

Interoperability should be preferred over unnecessary lock-in.

⸻

11. Decentralization

No single institution should be required to control every part of CCC.

Decentralization should be considered for:

* identity
* naming
* discovery
* hosting
* storage
* routing
* governance

Centralized infrastructure may still exist when it provides practical value.

The principle is not “centralization is always bad.”

The principle is:

Centralization should not be the only possible architecture.

⸻

12. Community Autonomy

Communities should have meaningful ability to establish their own standards.

A Christian community, scientific community, gaming community, privacy community, educational community, or other community may establish different rules.

CCC provides common infrastructure without requiring every community to become culturally identical.

⸻

13. Peaceful Competition

Communities, implementations, protocols, and services should be capable of competing.

If people disagree with an implementation, they should be able to:

* fork it
* replace it
* improve it
* create an alternative
* migrate to another implementation

Competition should occur through creation and participation rather than requiring control of the entire network.

⸻

14. Restoration

CCC should recognize that people and communities can make mistakes.

Where appropriate, systems should allow:

* correction
* apology
* reconciliation
* restoration
* rehabilitation
* revised reputation

A mistake should not necessarily become an immutable definition of a person’s entire identity.

⸻

15. Security

Freedom requires security.

CCC should protect participants against:

* unauthorized identity takeover
* malicious infrastructure
* data theft
* network attacks
* impersonation
* censorship through technical coercion
* manipulation of protocol records

Security should protect users without unnecessarily eliminating legitimate freedom.

⸻

16. Resilience

The network should continue functioning despite:

* individual nodes disappearing
* infrastructure failures
* organizations shutting down
* communities splitting
* software implementations being abandoned
* network disruptions

A decentralized system should not have a single point whose failure destroys the whole network.

⸻

17. Interoperability

CCC should prefer open protocols and documented interfaces.

Different implementations should be capable of communicating without requiring all participants to use the same software.

⸻

18. Evolution

CCC should be capable of changing.

Protocols should be versioned.

Experiments should be possible.

New implementations should be encouraged.

Existing assumptions should remain open to examination.

The project should not treat its first design as perfect.

⸻

19. Freedom of Exit

Participation should not become permanent captivity.

A person should be able to leave a service or community and retain as much control over their identity and data as technically and legally practical.

Likewise, communities should be able to separate from other communities.

⸻

20. The Commons

CCC should treat shared infrastructure as a commons that participants can contribute to, use, improve, and replace.

The commons belongs conceptually to the participating ecosystem rather than requiring one institution to own the entire system.

⸻

Guiding Principle

The principles of CCC can be summarized as:

Free people, responsible participation, voluntary community, mutual correction, shared infrastructure, and an open network.
