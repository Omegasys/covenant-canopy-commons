Covenant Canopy Commons

Human Model

1. Purpose

Covenant Canopy Commons is ultimately designed for human beings.

Before defining protocols, identities, nodes, or communities, CCC therefore needs a model of the human participant those systems are intended to serve.

This document describes the assumptions CCC makes about people.

These assumptions are philosophical rather than scientific claims about every individual.

⸻

2. The Individual

CCC begins with the individual human being.

A person should not be reduced to:

* an account
* an IP address
* a device
* a customer
* a reputation score
* a political identity
* a demographic category
* a dataset

A digital system may represent portions of a person’s identity.

It cannot fully represent the person.

⸻

3. Human Dignity

CCC treats human beings as possessing inherent dignity.

This means the network should be designed around people rather than treating people merely as resources for:

* advertising
* engagement
* data collection
* surveillance
* behavioral prediction
* centralized control

Technology should serve human beings.

Human beings should not be reduced to serving technology.

⸻

4. Freedom

CCC assumes that people possess meaningful capacity for choice.

People should be able to:

* create
* communicate
* associate
* disagree
* cooperate
* withdraw
* form communities
* leave communities
* establish alternatives

Freedom does not mean that every action must be free from consequences.

It means that meaningful choice should remain possible.

⸻

5. Responsibility

People are capable of making choices and therefore can have responsibilities associated with those choices.

CCC recognizes responsibility at multiple levels.

Individual responsibility

A person is responsible for their own actions and creations.

Community responsibility

A community is responsible for the standards and systems it establishes.

Infrastructure responsibility

Node operators and developers are responsible for the systems they intentionally operate.

Protocol responsibility

Protocol designers are responsible for understanding the consequences of the mechanisms they create.

⸻

6. People Are Relational

People do not exist only as isolated individuals.

They form:

* families
* friendships
* churches
* neighborhoods
* professional organizations
* communities
* cultures
* movements
* technical projects

CCC therefore treats relationships as an important component of digital life.

The goal is not simply to maximize individual autonomy.

It is to create an environment where autonomy and community can coexist.

⸻

7. People Are Different

CCC does not assume that everyone wants the same thing.

People differ in:

* beliefs
* personalities
* interests
* cultures
* values
* technical abilities
* communication styles
* privacy requirements
* community preferences

A decentralized system should therefore provide room for legitimate diversity.

⸻

8. People Can Cooperate

Human beings are capable of voluntarily cooperating toward shared goals.

CCC relies upon this possibility.

People can contribute:

* computing resources
* storage
* bandwidth
* software
* knowledge
* moderation
* documentation
* research
* financial resources
* community leadership

Participation should be voluntary wherever practical.

⸻

9. People Can Conflict

Human beings also disagree.

Conflict can arise from:

* competing beliefs
* conflicting interests
* misunderstandings
* misinformation
* personality differences
* resource limitations
* cultural differences

CCC therefore should not assume conflict can be eliminated.

Instead, the network should provide mechanisms for managing conflict without requiring universal centralized authority.

⸻

10. People Can Learn

CCC assumes that people can change their understanding.

They can:

* encounter evidence
* learn from others
* recognize mistakes
* change opinions
* develop skills
* become more knowledgeable

The network should therefore preserve opportunities for learning rather than treating every person as permanently defined by previous statements.

⸻

11. People Can Be Wrong

No individual or institution should be assumed to possess perfect knowledge.

This includes CCC itself.

The architecture should therefore permit:

* corrections
* revisions
* disagreement
* competing implementations
* alternative explanations
* protocol changes

A system that cannot acknowledge its own mistakes becomes vulnerable to institutional rigidity.

⸻

12. People Can Do Harm

Human freedom also creates the possibility of harmful behavior.

CCC must therefore account for:

* fraud
* harassment
* exploitation
* malicious software
* theft
* abuse
* manipulation
* coordinated attacks
* malicious infrastructure

Recognizing these risks does not require assuming that all people are malicious.

It means designing systems that remain resilient when some participants behave maliciously.

⸻

13. People Can Be Restored

CCC also assumes that people can change after making mistakes.

Accountability and restoration can coexist.

A person may:

1. make a mistake
2. acknowledge it
3. experience consequences
4. repair harm where possible
5. rebuild trust
6. participate again

The exact process should depend on the community and circumstances.

⸻

14. People Need Both Autonomy and Belonging

A healthy digital environment should not force a choice between:

“I am completely independent.”

and

“I belong completely to the group.”

People can be individuals while belonging to communities.

CCC therefore attempts to preserve:

Individual autonomy

and

Community belonging

simultaneously.

⸻

15. People Need the Ability to Leave

Participation is more meaningful when exit remains possible.

People should be able to leave communities and services without unnecessarily losing their entire digital identity.

This is why CCC emphasizes:

* portability
* interoperability
* identity ownership
* data ownership
* decentralized infrastructure

Exit is a component of freedom.

⸻

16. People Need Accountability

Freedom without accountability can become harmful to other people.

CCC therefore supports mechanisms through which communities can establish:

* standards
* expectations
* reputation
* moderation
* appeals
* consequences
* restoration

Accountability should be transparent and proportionate to the community’s legitimate authority.

⸻

17. People Need Privacy

People require spaces in which they can think, communicate, develop relationships, and make decisions without unnecessary observation.

CCC therefore treats privacy as part of human dignity and autonomy.

Privacy does not necessarily mean anonymity.

A person may choose to be:

* publicly identifiable
* pseudonymous
* anonymous
* privately identifiable to a trusted group

The appropriate level can vary by context.

⸻

18. People Need Truth

Human beings make decisions based on their understanding of reality.

Information systems therefore have an important relationship with truth.

CCC should support mechanisms that help participants distinguish:

* fact
* opinion
* interpretation
* speculation
* evidence
* allegation
* correction

The network cannot guarantee that everyone will agree on truth.

It can, however, provide mechanisms for examining claims.

⸻

19. People Need Other People

The phrase “iron sharpens iron” captures an important CCC assumption:

People can become stronger, wiser, and more capable through interaction with other people.

This interaction can involve:

* encouragement
* teaching
* criticism
* correction
* disagreement
* cooperation
* friendship

CCC therefore treats human-to-human interaction as a fundamental feature rather than merely traffic moving through a network.

⸻

20. People Are More Than Their Digital History

A person’s online history may contain valuable information, but it should not automatically become a permanent definition of the person.

CCC should therefore explore ways to distinguish:

Historical record

from

Permanent identity judgment.

Where appropriate, the system should allow context, correction, explanation, and restoration.

⸻

21. The Human Being at the Center

The final assumption of CCC is simple:

The network exists for people.

Protocols, cryptography, distributed databases, nodes, applications, communities, and governance mechanisms are tools.

They are not the ultimate purpose.

The purpose is to create an environment where human beings can:

* exercise freedom
* accept responsibility
* form communities
* protect privacy
* create
* communicate
* learn
* disagree
* correct one another
* forgive
* restore relationships
* participate in a shared commons

CCC should therefore continuously ask:

Does this technology increase meaningful human agency, or does it merely create another mechanism through which people become controlled by the system?

That question should remain present throughout the design of Covenant Canopy Commons.
